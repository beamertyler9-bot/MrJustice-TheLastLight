# Creature research notes

Reference notes gathered while redesigning the creatures, so each model is built on how the
real animal (or the animal it borrows from) is put together. Mr. Justice is 1.22 m tall; every
monster should stand clearly bigger than him.

## Windbell Plains (done)

### Stormhorn Matriarch (boss) - giant elk, *Megaloceros giganteus*
- Up to ~1.8-2.1 m at the shoulder in life, antlers spanning up to ~3.5 m: the span is roughly
  twice the shoulder height. The most long-legged, open-country deer.
- Cave art consistently shows a tall rounded shoulder hump (probably fat, no bone), sometimes
  with raised hairs (a "hackle tuft"), a lump on the throat (the larynx), a dark collar round
  the neck, a dark stripe behind the hump, and a dark diagonal stripe from shoulder to groin on
  a paler coat.
- Antlers sweep out sideways from the top of the skull: a forward brow tine, then the beam opens
  into a broad dished palm with tines along its back/outer edge.
- Applied: hump with a bristling hackle tuft, throat lump, collar and stripes (the shoulder
  stripe is a glowing lightning mark), long legs, palmate antlers with bolt-shaped tines, sparks
  between the tines and a lightning arc spanning the palms, fetlock rings of storm light and a
  flickering storm light at the antlers. Scaled to ~3.2 m at the shoulder, ~4.8 m to the tines.

### Stormfrill - horned ceratopsian (*Triceratops* / *Styracosaurus*)
- Triceratops: one of the largest skulls of any land animal, a parrot-like beak, three horns
  (keratin sheaths made them longer than the bone cores), pebbly scales, and a frill near 1 m
  across.
- Styracosaurus: a tall nose horn (~55-60 cm) and four long frill spikes (~50-55 cm) that point
  back, roughly parallel; a bulky rhino-like body, short tail, hoof-like claws.
- Applied: long brow horns plus a bigger nose horn, four long back-swept bolt spikes on the
  frill, small knobs round the rim, pebbled skin, a deep beak.

### Thunderclub - ankylosaur (*Ankylosaurus*)
- Low, very wide body; the skull is low, triangular and wider than long, with pyramidal horns
  pointing back and out from the rear corners and smaller cheek horns pointing back and down.
- Armour: flat keeled plates in transverse rows separated by skin creases, small ossicles in
  the gaps, smaller round plates over the hips, pointed plates on the hip and tail flanks, and
  two U-shaped half-rings of keeled plates over the neck. Big sideways body spikes are not known
  for *Ankylosaurus* itself.
- Tail club: semicircular from above - two big side lobes, a row of small knobs down the middle
  and two small ones at the tip; the last tail vertebrae stiffen into a handle.
- Applied: bands of plates with lightning in the creases, neck half-rings, pointed hip plates,
  only the two shoulder storm spikes kept for silhouette, the club rebuilt as above.

### Storm Bison, Thunder Rhino, Stormcloud Musk Ox
- Bison: head carried low, massive hump, long cape and beard, horns out then up.
- Indian rhino: heavy folds of hide with knobbly tubercles, three-toed feet, tufted ears.
- Musk ox: horn boss across the brow, horns down past the face then up, long skirt of guard
  hair to the knees, pale stockings.

## Coming up

### Sunspire Dunes
- **Obelisk Scorpion** - scorpion: the body is a cephalothorax (prosoma) and a seven-segment
  abdomen (mesosoma), then a five-segment tail (metasoma) and the bulb-and-sting telson. Big
  pincers (pedipalps with chelae) at the front, eight walking legs, the tail arched over the
  back. Thick-tailed, small-clawed species tend to be the most venomous.
- **Dune Maw** - antlion larva: digs a cone-shaped pit and waits buried at the bottom with
  only its huge sickle jaws showing; flicks sand at prey on the slope. A giant version could
  surface from a pit.
- **Glass Anubid** (boss) / hounds - jackal: long narrow muzzle, tall pointed ears, lean
  long-legged body, bushy tail.

### Ashfall Caldera
- **Ash Harpy** - harpy eagle: dark grey, a double crest that fans up when threatened, a grey
  facial disc like an owl's, legs as thick as a child's wrist, talons ~13 cm (longer than a
  grizzly's claws), short broad wings (~2 m span), stands ~0.9-1 m tall.

## Sandwraith, Dust Devil and Linen Revenant (Sunspire Dunes)
- Sandwraith: the face veil is modelled on the indigo tagelmust worn by desert travellers, wound so only an eye slit shows. The robe dissolves into a spinning column of sand, and it has a torn chest with a burning heart, gold cuffs and a brooch.
- Dust Devil: in Arabian folklore a whirling dust devil (zawba'a) is a djinn passing by. Its heart is a fulgurite, the glassy tube that lightning leaves when it strikes sand.
- Linen Revenant: a mummy in linen wrappings stained with resin. It has a gilded cartonnage funeral mask, a broad bead collar in gold, lapis and turquoise, and a heart scarab over the heart, as placed in real burials.

## Glass Anubid (Sunspire Dunes boss)
- Built as the god Anubis in humanoid form: a man's body with a jackal's head, as in Egyptian tomb paintings. It has the legs of the African golden wolf (the jackal the Egyptians drew Anubis from): long foot bones, a pointed heel and compact four-toed paws.
- Dressed after the Anubis statue from Tutankhamun's tomb (black body, gold-leaf brows, eye rims and ear linings, gold collar with a band, silver claws) and the gods' regalia in tomb paintings: striped wig, broad collar, kilt, was-sceptre and ankh.
- Sources: [Anubis Shrine (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anubis_Shrine), [Anubis Shrine of Tutankhamun](https://egypt-museum.com/anubis-shrine-of-tutankhamun)

## Riftback and Volcano Tortoise (Ashfall Caldera)
- Riftback: a giant pangolin. Real pangolins have a small smooth tapered head with no external ears, tiny heavy-lidded eyes and a very long tongue. They have about eighteen rows of broad, backward-pointing, overlapping scales over the back, flanks, outer legs and the whole top of the tail; the face, belly and inner legs are bare. Their front claws are huge, with the middle one largest, and are folded so the animal walks on the outer edges of its forefeet. The hind feet are broad and flat-soled with short claws. Sources: [Pholidota (Encyclopedia.com)](https://encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/pholidota-pangolins), [Pangolin (Britannica)](https://www.britannica.com/animal/pangolin)
- Volcano Tortoise: a Galápagos giant tortoise (they live on volcanic islands), with large horny scales on the forelegs and a domed shell of scutes. Here the shell is basalt carrying a small volcano.

## Furnace Hound, Cerberus and Bone Hound (Ashfall Caldera)
- These hellhounds are built on the grey wolf: long-legged and deep-chested, with an angled hind leg and a high hock, standing on four-toed paws with a stop above the eyes and a tapering muzzle. Cerberus follows the three-headed hound of Greek myth.

## Slag Crawler (Ashfall Caldera)
- A giant hermit crab in a turban-like spiralled shell, with one big crushing claw and one smaller claw, eyes on stalks, long antennae and two pairs of big walking legs. The shell and armour are slag: the glassy, rust-streaked crust that forms on cooling molten rock.

## Ash Harpy and Cinder Vulture (Ashfall Caldera)
- Ash Harpy: the harpy of Greek myth, with a vulture's body and wings and a gaunt hag's face.
- Cinder Vulture: built on the lappet-faced vulture. It has a bare head with folds of skin on the cheeks, a massive horn-coloured hooked beak, a ruff at the base of the neck, and long broad wings whose primaries spread like fingers at the tips.
- Both use vulture wings: about seven separated primary "fingers", long secondaries and layered coverts.

## Root Reaper, Grave Mandrake and Bog Wight (Hollowmourn)
- Root Reaper: built on the strangler fig, whose aerial roots wrap a host tree and fuse where they touch until the host rots away and leaves a hollow lattice.
- Grave Mandrake: the mandrake of folklore, a forked man-shaped root. The real plant has a rosette of large wrinkled leaves and round yellow-orange fruit.
- Bog Wight: built on bog oak, black oak preserved for centuries in peat. It is hung with Spanish moss, has bracket fungi on its shoulders, and glows with foxfire, the bioluminescent fungus of rotting wood.

## Wraithhart (Hollowmourn)
- Built on the red deer stag, dead and still walking. A deer skull is long and tapered, with a nasal opening along the top of the snout, round eye sockets set on the sides behind a raised bony rim, and a cheekbone arch running back beneath them.
- Deer have no upper front teeth; the cheek teeth (premolars and molars) sit in rows along the back of the jaw.
- The withers are high because the vertebrae over the shoulders have tall spines. The pelvis wings and the shoulder blades show through a starving animal's hide.
- The antlers follow a "royal" red-deer head (brow, bez and trez tines, then a crown). Stags shed the velvet in late summer and it peels off in long, bloody strips.

## Hollow Hound and Shadow Wolf (Hollowmourn)
- Hollow Hound: Black Shuck, the "church grim" of East Anglian folklore, a huge shaggy black dog with eyes like burning saucers; the barghest of northern England drags a chain. Its build follows the Irish wolfhound: very tall, long-legged and deep-chested, with a long head and a wiry coat that forms a beard and eyebrows.
- Shadow Wolf: built on the grey wolf (long legs, deep narrow chest, pointed ears, a long bushy tail), made of shadow so its skull, ribs, spine and leg bones show inside.
- Considered but not used: the thylacine (Tasmanian tiger), extinct since 1936. It had 13-21 dark bands across the back and rump, a stiff tail thick at the root, and jaws that could gape very wide.

## Grave Creeper and Grave Mantis (Hollowmourn)
- Grave Creeper: built on the assassin bugs (Reduviidae). Some, like Acanthaspis petax, pile the emptied husks of their prey and debris on their backs as camouflage. They have a narrow head with bulging eyes behind a long neck region, elbowed antennae, a curved three-part beak folded under the head, a flared abdomen edge (the connexivum), and often banded legs.
- Grave Mantis: built on the praying mantis. The long prothorax forms the "neck"; the raptorial forelegs carry rows of spines on the femur and tibia and fold together; the leathery forewings (tegmina) cover the membranous hind wings. Details come from relatives: the broad pronotum of the shield mantises, the head crest of the conehead mantises (Empusa), and the leg and abdomen lobes of the ghost mantis.
- Considered but not used: the Japanese spider crab (the longest legs of any arthropod), which, like decorator crabs, covers itself with things it finds.

## Veil Moth, Death's-head Moth and Ghost Moon Moth (Hollowmourn)
- Death's-head hawkmoth (Acherontia atropos): a skull-like pale pattern on the thorax, narrow dark mottled forewings, yellow hindwings with black bands, and a yellow-and-black banded abdomen with a blue-grey stripe down the back. It squeaks when disturbed and raids beehives for honey.
- Giant silk moths (the polyphemus moth, Automeris io and the owl moths) flash big eyespots on their hindwings to startle predators. The rings run black, gold and pale blue around a dark pupil with a pale highlight. The males have broad feathery (bipectinate) antennae.
- Moon moths (the luna moth Actias luna and the comet moth Argema mittrei) are pale green with long twisting hindwing tails, which confuse bats' echolocation, and small eyespot "windows". The luna moth has a dusky purple-brown leading edge on each forewing.
- The combined Veil Moth was tried and removed; the Death's-head Moth takes its place.

## Thorn Widow and Spiny Orb-weaver (Hollowmourn)
- Spider anatomy used for both: a cephalothorax carrying eight legs, eight eyes in rows with the front middle pair largest, chelicerae with fangs, and a pair of pedipalps; a narrow waist (pedicel); the abdomen with spinnerets at its tip. Each leg runs coxa, femur, patella (the high "knee"), tibia, metatarsus and tarsus.
- Thorn Widow: built on Darwin's bark spider (Caerostris darwini) of Madagascar. It spins the largest orb webs known, with bridge lines across whole rivers. It is bark-coloured, with a knobbly abdomen and two humps at the front.
- Spiny Orb-weaver: built on Gasteracantha and its kin. The abdomen is a hard, wide, flat plate with six spines around the edge and pale dimples (sigilla) on top, set on a small body.
- Considered but not used: the black widow (Latrodectus), glossy black with a red hourglass under the abdomen.

## Mourning Knight (Hollowmourn), and how figures hold things
- A knight's harness, head to foot: a helm (the flat-topped great helm of the 1200s; the sallet with its long tail, worn with a bevor at the chin; the visored armet with a rondel behind), a gorget, a breastplate with a plackart over the belly, the fauld (overlapping lames over the hips) with tassets hanging over the thighs, pauldrons, rerebraces, couters with side fans, vambraces, gauntlets with flared cuffs, cuisses, poleyns with fans, greaves and overlapping sabatons. Mail goes where plate can't reach (armpits, groin), or makes up the whole hauberk and coif on an earlier knight. Over the armour: a surcoat or tabard bearing the knight's arms, and a cloak. Gothic armour (Germany, about 1480) is fluted, with cusped, scalloped edges and long pointed sabatons.
- Heraldry of mourning: "gouttes de larmes" (silver teardrops strewn on a field) and the hatchment, the lozenge of arms hung up when someone died.
- Heater shield: wood faced with leather, flat-topped with curved sides meeting in a point. It was held on the forearm by straps, with a guige (a long strap round the neck) to carry it.
- Holding a sword (from the fencing manuals): the grip runs through the closed fist with the index finger by the crossguard and the pommel below the little finger. The thumb closes over the fingers, the last three fingers hold firm, and the wrist stays in line with the forearm. The blade leaves the fist on the thumb side with its edges in line with the knuckles, and the crossguard lies along the edges. A two-handed sword has both hands close together, the second hand at or just above the pommel.
- Guards: Vom Tag (sword held high by the shoulder), Ochs (hilt by the head, point at the face), Pflug (hilt by the hip, point at the chest), Alber (point down in front). A knight keeping vigil stands with the sword point-down in front of him, both hands resting on the hilt.
- Posing anything held: build the palm as a block first, set the object through it, then wrap the fingers round it with pressure on the inside of the grip. A held weapon should look as if it has weight; it shouldn't look glued on or floating free of the arm.
- Sources: Wikipedia, "List of medieval armour components" and "Heater shield"; swordfight.uk, "Gripping the sword"; ARMA, "The Basic Guards of Medieval Longsword"; PoseMyArt, "Drawing weapon-holding poses".
- Chosen design: the black knight, pushed toward fantasy. Its grotesque visor follows real grotesque-mask helmets such as Henry VIII's horned helmet of 1512, and its sword is a flamberge (a real wavy-bladed sword). The ram's-curl horns sweep back and down rather than up, so the head doesn't read as cat ears.

## Pale Watcher (Hollowmourn boss)
- Built on cemetery angel statues and the "veiled" marbles of 18th- and 19th-century Italy (Giuseppe Sanmartino's Veiled Christ in Naples; Giovanni Strazza's Veiled Virgin). In those, a thin carved veil lies over a face you can still make out beneath it. The figure has deep-folded robes, folded wings, a halo, and a handbell held by its handle.
- Its gaze is a pale beam fired from the mask's eyes behind the veil.
- Considered but not used: a cracked bronze church bell with a hooded figure in a Venetian bauta mask rising from it; and the ophanim of Ezekiel 1:18 (wheels whose "rims were full of eyes round about") with the six wings of Isaiah's seraphim.

## Blackthorn Regent (Hollowmourn boss)
- Built on the same researched harness as the Mourning Knight: a close helmet with a pointed visor, a tarnished crown and a lantern of iron and horn held by its ring.
- Blackthorn (Prunus spinosa): black bark; short side-shoots that harden into long spines; white five-petalled blossom (which opens before the leaves); round blue-black sloes. It forms dense, impenetrable thickets.
- The king died in his armour and blackthorn grew up through it, bursting out of the breastplate and every gap in the plate. His antlers are blackthorn branches.
- Considered but not used: a king in black parade armour etched with gold (like the Emperor Maximilian's), with an ermine tippet; a great-helmed tournament king.

## Shard Sentinel (Prismere)
- Built on selenite (crystalline gypsum) as found in the Cave of the Crystals at Naica, Mexico, where beams reach over 11 m long. Selenite crystals are long, flat-sided prisms of roughly rectangular section that end in slanted, chisel-like faces. They are pearly and translucent with fine lengthwise striations, and they grow out of dark cave rock crusted orange and black with iron and manganese oxides.
- The head is a split geode: a rock shell whose hollow is lined with crystals pointing inward.
- A maul is held with both hands on the haft, well apart, the head carried over the shoulder; its head is set across the haft in the plane of the swing.
- Considered but not used: rock-crystal quartz (six-sided prisms with six-faced points, in druses on granite, with amethyst); bismuth hopper crystals (stair-stepped hollow squares with iridescent oxide).

## Prismstalker (Prismere) — snow leopard

Built on the snow leopard (*Panthera uncia*): pale smoky-grey coat, paler underneath; a deep chest and short, strong forelegs; a small, rounded head; and a very thick tail almost as long as its body, which it uses for balance and wraps around itself for warmth. Its rosettes are open dark rings rather than solid spots. In the Prismere each rosette is a dark smoky ring holding split rainbow light, like the colour fringes thrown by a prism. Small frost crystals run along the spine. The game's existing cue is kept: a steady white glint runs along the ridge of the back before it pounces.

## Geode Mimic, Celestine Box Crab, Thunder Egg Crab (Prismere) — crabs living in geodes

**Geodes.** A geode is a hollow, roughly round rock. Its outside is a knobbly, "cauliflower" rind of chalcedony or limestone. Just inside the rind is a band of agate in thin concentric stripes, and the hollow is lined with crystals that all point inwards: amethyst (purple), celestine (pale sky blue, famous from Madagascar) or smoky quartz and citrine. A "thunder egg" is a geode that formed in volcanic rock, so its rind is dark basalt or rhyolite.

**Crab anatomy.**
- Each walking leg has seven segments: coxa, basis, ischium, then the long, flattened merus, which rises to the "knee". After it come the carpus, the propodus and a pointed dactyl that grips the ground.
- The legs are fringed with bristles (setae).
- The claws (chelipeds) close a movable finger (the dactyl) onto a fixed finger that grows out of the swollen palm (propodus).
- Around the mouth is a fan of layered mouthparts (maxillipeds), and the eyes sit on stalks.

**The three designs.**
- **Geode Mimic:** an amethyst geode on a shore crab's legs. The claws are folded under the seam.
- **Celestine Box Crab:** based on the box crab (*Calappa*, the "shame-faced crab"). It hides its face behind two huge flat claws that look like part of its shell, each topped with a cockscomb crest.
- **Thunder Egg Crab:** based on the Japanese spider crab (*Macrocheira kaempferi*), which has the longest legs of any arthropod. Its legs are spiny and orange, and it carries the thunder egg high above the ground.

**Creepy touches shared by all three.**
- Two thin slits of glowing eyes watch through the seam while the shell is shut.
- Inside are too many stalked eyes with slit pupils.
- The gullet is a wet red pit with mouthparts around it.
- Drool hangs from the lid's teeth.
- Bones and a small skull lie among the crystals.
